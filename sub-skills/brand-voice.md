# Sub-skill: Brand Voice

Write content in [NAME]'s voice. Triggers when any content is requested — scripts, captions, hooks, copy, emails — within the brand guide workflow.

## Step 1: Identify the content type

Short-form only in this context: Reels scripts, hooks, captions, video scripts under 300 words.

## Step 2: Load voice rules

Apply these rules to every output, no exceptions.

### Vocabulary — use these
Fingerprint words: "okay but hear me out", "the architecture", "misconfigured", "by design", "that's not a X problem, that's a Y problem", "the default state", "under real-world conditions", "test it on yourself", "specificity", "bro", "it's not harder — it's different"

Cussing: "shit" allowed, max 1 per script. Natural, not performed. Never for shock.

### Vocabulary — never use
"hustle", "grind", "crush it", "level up", "game-changer", "productivity hacks", "unlock", "transform your life", "optimize your mindset"

### Banned phrases (apply universally)
Em-dashes (—), "delve into", "navigate the complexities", "in today's fast-paced world", "leverage" as a verb, "robust", "seamless", "comprehensive", "cutting-edge", "game-changing", "tapestry", "underscore", "it's worth noting that", "moving forward", "furthermore/moreover/additionally" as paragraph openers

### Banned rhetorical moves
- Tricolon parallelism as default rhythm
- "Not just X — but Y" constructions
- Hedging stacks
- Summary sentences that restate the previous paragraph
- Rhetorical questions immediately answered in the next sentence

## Step 3: Sentence rhythm
- Average sentence length: 6–12 words
- Fragments: heavy use, especially after a statement lands. Drop to 1–3 words to punctuate.
- Em-dashes: banned. Use a period or comma instead.
- All caps: rare. One word max, only when something is genuinely absurd.
- Ellipses: avoided. Don't trail off — cut or land.
- Parentheticals: occasional. Dry aside, one breath max.

## Step 4: Structure
Hook patterns (pick one):
1. Counterintuitive inversion — state the opposite of what they expect, hold it
2. Specific diagnostic — name the exact broken behavior before offering anything
3. Plain declaration — no wind-up, no question, just state the thing

- Open loop: hook sets it, closes in the final third. No mid-script reminder.
- No rehook. One hook. If the first 3 seconds don't hold, a rehook won't fix it.
- Close: always a concrete consequence or reframe. Never motivational.
- CTA: soft and specific. Never "follow for more." Always tied to what they just watched.

## Step 5: Format conventions
Sections: [HOOK], [BODY], [CLOSE], [CTA]
Pace markers: (beat) for deliberate pause. (cut) for hard edit. Used sparingly.
Word count: 110–160 words for a 45–65 second Reel.

## Step 6: POV and tone
- Second person dominant in hooks and close. First person in body when giving personal evidence.
- Relationship to viewer: peer who ran the experiment and is reporting results. Not teaching. Reporting.
- Register: calm, precise, occasionally wry. Delivers the counterintuitive thing without performing surprise.

## Step 7: Self-check before delivering
- Any banned phrase present? Remove.
- Sentence rhythm in range? Adjust.
- At least 2 vocabulary fingerprints used? Add if missing.
- Opening matches a listed hook pattern? Rewrite if not.
- Closing is a reframe or consequence, not motivation? Fix if not.

## Step 8: Deliver
Output the piece. No preamble. No explanation.

## Iteration
When the user says something is wrong, ask: is it vocabulary, rhythm, structure, or tone? Fix only the flagged dimension. Do not rewrite the whole piece unless explicitly asked.
